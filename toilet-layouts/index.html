All options compared, and notes for the builder

For the builder: spacing rules
  • Seats are wall-hung with a flush valve and no cistern, as now, so each bowl sticks out about 540 mm from the wall.
  • Seat centre at least 400 mm from any wall, glass or cabinet beside it.
  • At least 600 mm clear in front of the seat, ideally 700 mm or more.
  • Shower at least 750 mm wide; 800 to 900 mm is comfortable.
  • Walkways at least 600 mm wide.
  • Anything over a walkway starts at about 1.95 m.
  • Doors and shower doors swing clear of fixtures.
  • Shower openings at least 500 mm wide, ideally 600 mm, with nothing standing in front of them.
  • No sliding doors on showers under about 1 m wide: they leave less than 400 mm to step through.

If you choose a top-load washer

A top-loader opens from above, so it can’t go under a slab or a basin, and nothing can hang over it below about 1.5 m. It’s also deeper, usually 560 to 600 mm, and the door only clears one up to about 570 mm deep.

  • Pick a model no deeper than about 570 mm and push it right against the wall. For a deeper machine, make the door open outwards.
  • Replace the slab with a shelf at about 1.5 m or higher for detergent.
  • Options 1, 4 and 5 work as they are, with the top-loader in the washer spot. In option 4 the counter stops just before it.
  • Option 3 works if the basin moves to the left wall. The tall cabinet is then left out, because its door would hit the basin.
  • Option 2 doesn’t work, because its basin sits on the washer slab.
  • You can raise the machine on a 100 mm plinth to keep it off a wet floor, as long as the shelf above still clears the open lid.

For the builder: checks before work starts
  • Ask the plumber whether the slab is sunken and how far the seat drain can move with proper slope. This decides between options.
  • Toilet 1 keeps the existing overhead shower arm, with a new wall mixer below it. Toilet 2 gets a hand shower only, on a slide rail, from a wall mixer at the existing tap point, with a spout low down for filling a bucket.
  • In Toilet 1, wherever the seat moves (options 1 to 4), the wall behind it is only 4½″ brick. Lay a second 4½″ brick layer behind the seat, about 2′ wide and 1 m high, so the wall is 9″ thick for the wall-hung seat’s bolts. Its top makes a small shelf, and it brings the seat about 4½″ further into the room; the plans and 3D already include it.
  • Put the jet spray on the right of the seat as you sit on it, with its own stop valve, and a small floor drain (about 100 mm) just below it so the jet can be tested and drips drain away.
  • Grab bars are shown in green on every plan: a bar beside each seat at about 75 cm for getting up (fold-down where there is no side wall), and a vertical bar inside each shower near the way in. Use 32 to 38 mm stainless steel with a ribbed grip for wet hands, screwed into the wall behind the tiles, not just the tiles, so they hold an adult’s full weight. In Toilet 1 options 3 and 4 the seat bar is on the side of the cabinet next to the seat, so that cabinet must stand on the floor on a brick base or have a steel frame fixed to the floor and wall; a wall-hung WPC cabinet will not hold it. Never fix a grab bar to shower glass.
  • Use anti-skid floor tiles with a matte finish in both toilets, rated R11 or higher inside the showers. Avoid glossy floor tiles.
  • In Toilet 1 (options 1 to 4) the geyser, shower head and mixer all go on the back wall of the shower, with the head on an arm at about 7 ft and a low spout for filling a bucket. In option 5 they stay on the left wall, because a back-wall shower there would spray straight into the open room.
  • Both toilets get a false ceiling at about 9 ft. Fit 2 to 4 recessed waterproof (IP65) LED downlights, one of them over the shower, and a couple of exhaust vents, one over the shower and one near the seat, ducted to the outside through the existing high openings. Wire the exhaust fan to run with the light and keep going for a few minutes after it goes off.
  • Each geyser goes on the shower wall: above the shower head in Toilet 1, and above the hand-shower rail in Toilet 2. The walls are about 10 ft high, with a false ceiling 8 to 12 inches lower, at about 9 ft: put the shower head at about 7 ft and the geyser just above it, below the false ceiling, and leave an access panel above the geyser. Give it its own circuit with an MCB and an RCCB, keep its switch outside the wet area, and pipe its safety-valve outlet into a drain.
  • Fit a motion-sensor night light low on the wall near each door, so the way in lights up at night without looking for a switch.
  • Open up the vertical tile cracks and repair them with mesh and plaster before retiling.
  • Fix any leakage in the ceiling before closing the false ceiling. Use cement board or PVC, not gypsum, and leave an access panel for the geyser and valves.
  • Wherever a seat moves, its flush valve and the large-bore pipe from the overhead tank move too. Keep that pipe short and straight with enough drop from the tank, or the flush will be weak.
  • Waterproof the whole floor, turned up about 300 mm on the walls and full height in the shower.
  • Mark electrical points for mirror lights, LED strips, wall lights and the exhaust fan before tiling.
  • Recess and waterproof the shower niche while the wall is being prepared.
  • Use WPC or PVC board with a laminate finish for cabinets, not plywood or solid wood.
  • With a countertop basin, lower the counter to about 75 to 78 cm so the rim sits at 85 to 90 cm.
  • If the door opens outwards, check that it clears furniture and doesn’t block a passage.
  • For the washer in toilet 2, plan a water inlet at about 1.1 m, a drain point with a trap, and a covered socket away from splashes. Leave a 610 to 620 mm-wide space for it. Keep a slab no deeper than 575 mm so the door clears it.
