All options compared, and notes for the builder

For the builder: spacing rules
  • WCs are wall-hung with a flush valve and no cistern, as now, so each bowl sticks out about 540 mm from the wall.
  • WC centre at least 400 mm from any wall, glass or cabinet beside it.
  • At least 600 mm clear in front of the WC, ideally 700 mm or more.
  • Shower at least 750 mm wide; 800 to 900 mm is comfortable.
  • Walkways at least 600 mm wide.
  • Anything over a walkway starts at about 1.95 m.
  • Doors and shower doors swing clear of fixtures.
  • Shower openings at least 500 mm wide, ideally 600 mm, with nothing standing in front of them.
  • No sliding doors on showers under about 1 m wide: they leave less than 400 mm to step through.

If you choose a top-load washer

A top-loader opens from above, so it can’t go under a slab or a basin, and nothing can hang over it below about 1.5 m. It’s also deeper, usually 560 to 600 mm, and the door only clears one up to about 570 mm deep.

  • Pick a model no deeper than about 570 mm and push it right against the wall. For a deeper machine, make the door open outwards.
  • Replace the slab with a shelf at about 1.5 m or higher for detergent.
  • Options 1, 4 and 5 work as they are, with the top-loader in the washer spot. In option 4 the counter stops just before it.
  • Option 3 works if the basin moves to the left wall, in place of the tall cabinet.
  • Option 2 doesn’t work, because its basin sits on the washer slab.
  • You can raise the machine on a 100 mm plinth to keep it off a wet floor, as long as the shelf above still clears the open lid.

For the builder: checks before work starts
  • Ask the plumber whether the slab is sunken and how far the WC drain can move with proper slope. This decides between options.
  • Open up the vertical tile cracks and repair them with mesh and plaster before retiling.
  • Fix any leakage in the ceiling before closing the false ceiling. Use cement board or PVC, not gypsum, and leave an access panel for the geyser and valves.
  • Wherever a WC moves, its flush valve and the large-bore pipe from the overhead tank move too. Keep that pipe short and straight with enough drop from the tank, or the flush will be weak.
  • Waterproof the whole floor, turned up about 300 mm on the walls and full height in the shower.
  • Mark electrical points for mirror lights, LED strips, wall lights and the exhaust fan before tiling.
  • Recess and waterproof the shower niche while the wall is being prepared.
  • Use WPC or PVC board with a laminate finish for cabinets, not plywood or solid wood.
  • With a countertop basin, lower the counter to about 75 to 78 cm so the rim sits at 85 to 90 cm.
  • If the door opens outwards, check that it clears furniture and doesn’t block a passage.
  • For the washer in toilet 2, plan a water inlet at about 1.1 m, a drain point with a trap, and a covered socket away from splashes. Leave a 610 to 620 mm-wide space for it. Keep a slab no deeper than 575 mm so the door clears it.
